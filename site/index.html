Who it's for

Owners with roughly 3 to 30 rental doors, managed by an outside property manager, or by two or three different ones.

The moment is January to March. Your CPA asks for the year's rental numbers, and what you have is twelve PDF statements per property, laid out differently by each manager, often with rent shown net of fees, repairs tucked into "other", and deposits that don't obviously match your bank.

I do the sorting, matching and adding up. Your CPA gets clean totals per property, with every open question listed in one place.

What you get

One Excel workbook (it also opens in Google Sheets) covering calendar year 2026. Every total is a live formula your CPA can trace.

  1. Summary. Owner, year, each property's totals, a status line, and the bookkeeping-not-tax-advice disclaimer.
  2. Ledger, one per property. Month by month: gross rent collected, management fee, leasing fee, repairs, other PM charges and net owner distribution. Gross rent and fees are kept separate, never netted.
  3. Schedule E map. Each property's annual totals placed against the 2026 Schedule E line they would usually go on, for your CPA to confirm.
  4. Bank tie-out. Each month's PM distribution against the deposit in your bank export, with any difference highlighted.
  5. Exceptions. The items your CPA should look at: deposits that don't match, missing months, unexplained charges, and expenses you paid yourself outside the PM.
  6. Contractors. Every payee and their 2026 total, with anyone paid $2,000 or more flagged so your CPA can review 1099-NEC filing.

Questions

Which files do I send?
  • Every monthly owner statement from each property manager for January to December 2026 (PDF or CSV). A year-end summary from the PM also helps, if they provide one.
  • A bank export (CSV, or PDF statements) for January to December 2026 from the account the PM pays into.
  • Receipts or a short list of anything you paid yourself, outside the PM (for example insurance or a repair you paid directly).
What if a month is missing?

I'll tell you which statements are missing as soon as I've checked your files, so you can ask your PM for them. If a statement can't be found, I note the month on the Exceptions sheet, along with any bank deposit that has no statement behind it, so your CPA can see exactly what is and isn't covered. The 5-business-day clock starts when the documents are complete.

My properties have different property managers. Is that a problem?

No. Each property gets its own ledger whatever its manager's statement looks like, and the bank tie-out matches each manager's payments separately.
