# Principles of good motion, and remix operators

These principles come from 82 big-brand motion breakdowns, plus an earlier catalog. Every public link below is an official brand video; every number was measured (ffmpeg scene detection, EBU R128 loudness, librosa onsets). Sound was measured, not listened to.

How to use this file:

1. Pick the **use** (hook, feature callout, explainer, social proof, end card).
2. Pick **two principles** the moment needs (for example, a hook often wants anticipation and contrast).
3. Pick **one or two technique families** in `technique-families.md`, read why they work, then run the **invention prompts** and the **remix operators** below until you have a move none of the references made.
4. Build it with the After Effects, Blender or code recipe, then run the checklist in `preflight-checklist.md` before you storyboard or render.
5. Design the sound with `premium-sound.md`. Cheap, generic sound effects are the clearest tell of AI-made motion.

Counts in brackets are how many of the 82 breakdowns named the principle in their "Why it works" section. They show what recurs, not a ranking of importance.

## The ten principles

### 1. Continuity: change one thing at a time (31 breakdowns)

Keep something constant (an object, a ground, a type voice, the camera) while the rest changes; the change becomes the message. Evidence: [Adobe Firefly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fjk2mMFBpRo) carries one jellyfish image from full frame → app → laptop; [Coca-Cola Masterpiece](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VGa1imApfdg) hands one bottle across art styles; [AirPods Pro 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EMmKs8vMKhU) uses one ring as sound, callout, tunnel and logo. Measured: 23 of 82 videos run at ≤0.5 scene cuts per 10s (for example [Apple Creator Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ISYIEmQxs2M), 0.3 cuts/10s, −16.1 LUFS; [Microsoft Fluent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNvDUe73F50), 0.4 cuts/10s, −17.6 LUFS). They change content by moves and morphs instead of cuts.

**Test:** what stays the same across this cut? If nothing, add a constant.

### 2. Clarity: one idea per beat (27)

One word, one control, one number on screen at a time; isolate the thing that matters and give it room. Evidence: [Apple Creator Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ISYIEmQxs2M) (one white word per beat on black), [Raycast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mi173xGb0ZA) (one UI piece floating alone), [Figma Slides](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p-B--SGq_ZQ) (push in until one item fills the frame).

**Test:** can a muted viewer say what this shot is about in three words?

### 3. Restraint: fewer things, longer (25)

Limited palettes, small type, quiet mixes and long holds read as confidence. Evidence: median pace is **1.85 cuts per 10s** (quartiles 0.5–3.92); loudness median **−15.4 LUFS** (quartiles −18.5 to −13.2), and several mix much quieter: Anthropic thinking partner −26.4, Cursor 2.0 −29.3, Duolingo three words −25.0, Pixel Watch 2 −22.6, Pixel 8 −22.4, Microsoft 365 Copilot −25.1 LUFS. [Cursor 2.0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=An8IM-kPyms) is paper, black type and one dark bar at −29.3 LUFS; [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VHsp6Hp7Stw) is two colours plus ink.

**Test:** remove one element. Is the shot worse? If not, leave it out.

### 4. Contrast: make the hero the odd one out (24)

Colour, scale, light or era contrast tells the eye where to look. Evidence: [MacBook Air](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y9ITR44W5s) (one dark chip shot in a white film), [Netflix Planeswalker](https://x.com/netflix/status/2121318431139967161) (one warm flame in a cold frame), [Arc for Windows](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LJQsAOon6og) (90s pixels vs modern UI), [Coca-Cola Holidays](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yy6fByUmPuE) (toy truck → real truck).

**Test:** squint at the frame. Is the brightest or most saturated thing the hero?

### 5. Anticipation → payoff: set it up, then pay it off (23)

Delay, a countdown, half a sentence or a typing cursor create a question; the reveal answers it. Evidence: [Duolingo × Squid Game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o3ftECYtkf0) delays Duo's face about 10 s (silhouette → feet → face); [Pixel 9 Pro Fold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NC_A3EHFKF4) types a breakup letter before showing any phone; [Airbnb Icons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rz2FWiJq4Mw) opens on a countdown pill.

**Test:** what is the viewer waiting for at second 2, and when do they get it?

### 6. Rhythm: pattern + variation (17)

A fixed template (caption, layout, alternation) with one changing element makes many shots feel like one sentence. Evidence: [Google 25 Years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3KtWfp0UopM) (fixed caption, swapping word over 148 cuts), [MacBook Air](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y9ITR44W5s) (text card / product alternation), [Arc Boosts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0jMmiCS8oQ) (same layout, new skin each cut). Fast end: seven videos cut at ≥8 per 10s (Copilot companion 14.9, Adobe MAX 13.6, Arc for Windows 10.4, McDonald's WcDonald's 9.8), all sizzles or montages with one type system on top.

**Test:** can you name the pattern? Where does it break, and is that break the payoff?

### 7. Character: give the action an actor (17)

A face (even a dot ring or a blob) doing the task is warmer and easier to follow than UI doing it. Evidence: [Duolingo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IEHiY8scAKI) (plush 3D mascot with eye acting), [Anthropic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VHsp6Hp7Stw) (faceless shape, many roles), [Airtable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UcZfSIegPNw) (dot ring = the AI's face and its loader), [Claude meetings](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2120973994191094179) (status circle acts out the task).

**Test:** who is doing this? If the answer is "the UI", try giving it to the character.

### 8. Concrete metaphor: show the benefit as a thing (13)

Abstract benefits become physical props or literal actions. Evidence: [Headspace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wyj8l9miy4w) (effort = a rope you drop), [Headspace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mb19Ee8Dino) ("learn to surf" acted out), [LottieFiles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_sjNPSA4kg) (heavy animals = heavy files), [McDonald's](https://x.com/McDonalds/status/2122359723668222462) (a receipt printer delivers the teaser).

**Test:** is there a progress bar, spinner or plain checkmark? Replace it with an object doing something.

### 9. Proof: show the output, not the claim (9)

Real outputs, real typing at reading speed, one number that moves. Evidence: [Notion AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FElBbgnNtVA) (live streaming text), [Framer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6xJWQRVl9nw) (wall of real sites), [Lottie AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jUEUaCFINro) (zoom into the vector to prove crispness), [GitHub Octoverse](https://x.com/github/status/2121689749043785812) (11%+ → 40%+ counting card).

**Test:** what in this frame would convince a sceptic?

### 10. Sound sync: sync the key actions, not every cut (4)

Measured across all 1,710 scene cuts in the 82-video set, **52.4%** land within 70 ms of an audio onset, against **50.8%** expected by chance. So big-brand edits, on the whole, do **not** lock every cut to the music. The strongest above-chance examples are [MacBook Air](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y9ITR44W5s) (13/20 vs 45%), [Duolingo × Squid Game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o3ftECYtkf0) (6/15 vs 22%), [iPad Air](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oT5iDm9XRl8) (13/28 vs 31%) and [Google 25 Years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3KtWfp0UopM) (63/148 vs 33%); [Coca-Cola Holidays](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yy6fByUmPuE) cuts **off** the beat on purpose (1/23 vs 13%). The lesson: choose a few hero actions (the stamp, the logo, the reveal) and hit them exactly with real sound effects and music; let the rest breathe. Limits: onsets were only compared with scene cuts, so in-shot hits (a pop, a stamp) are not in these numbers, and nobody listened to the tracks.

**Test:** list the 3–5 moments that must land on a hit. Does each have a real sound?

## Remix operators

Use these on a reference move to get something new that keeps the same principle. Learn from the references; do not copy them.

| Operator | What you change | Example from the reference set → a new move |
|---|---|---|
| Swap the actor | Who performs the move | Typed prompt (Pixel 9 Pro Fold) → a mascot's eyes become the caret and it types. |
| Swap the scale | Tiny ↔ huge, inline ↔ full screen | Inline glyph in a sentence (Apple Creator Studio) → the inline object grows to near full screen at an angle. |
| Make it literal | The metaphor becomes a prop | Progress bar → logos poured in as fuel; checkmark → a rubber "done" stamp. |
| Reverse it | Run the move backwards or end-first | Logo built from a motif (Netflix ribbons) → open on the logo and let it unravel into the story. |
| Constrain it | One take, one colour, one word | Zero-cut (Pixel 9 Pro, Pitch) + one accent colour → a single-take desktop where only the character uses the accent colour. |
| Combine two families | Stack moves from different families on one beat | Pull-back reveal + gag (Pixel 9 Pro Fold) → pull back from one email to reveal the character sending forty of them. |
| Move the medium | Do a UI move with a physical object, or vice versa | Receipt printer teaser (McDonald's) → the character's reply prints out of a laptop like a receipt. |
| Give it a cost | Make the character struggle before it succeeds | Headspace surfer → the character wobbles under a pile of emails before it sorts them. |
